01 / THE INVISIBLE

SMALL DOESN'T MEAN SIMPLE

Meet the life
you missed.

Microorganisms are living things so tiny that most can only be seen with a microscope. They live in water, air, soil—and even inside our bodies.

Some help us make food and keep nature healthy. Others can cause disease or spoil food. Let’s take a closer look.

02 / A CLOSER LOOK

ONE CELL. MANY MOVES.

Meet the Amoeba.

Amoeba is a single-celled microorganism. It changes shape to move and capture food. Tap a glowing part to learn what it does.

SELECT A STRUCTURE
CELL STRUCTURE 01
◉

Cell membrane

A thin outer boundary that surrounds the cell and helps control what enters and leaves.

WHY IT MATTERS

It separates the cell from its surroundings.

1 / 5
✳ CLASS 8 NOTE

Amoebas do not have a fixed shape. They form temporary finger-like extensions called pseudopodia (“false feet”) to move and surround food.

04 / THE EVERYDAY SCIENCE

THE KITCHEN IS A LAB

Tiny workers.
Big flavour.

Yeast is a microorganism used in baking. It feeds on sugar and releases carbon dioxide gas. The gas gets trapped in dough, making it rise.

05 / PLANET-SIZED IMPACT

THE WORK YOU DON'T SEE

Nature runs on
tiny teams.

Microorganisms help keep ecosystems working. Explore two important jobs.

✳
01 / SOIL CREW

Decomposition

Some bacteria and fungi break down dead plants and animals. Nutrients return to the soil and can be used again.

N₂
02 / ROOT PARTNERS

Nitrogen fixation

Some bacteria in the root nodules of leguminous plants help change nitrogen from air into forms plants can use.
